WHY THIS WEBSITE EXISTS

About the record book

The SHSU Rocket League Record Book is an independent community archive of recorded competitive matches involving Sam Houston State University Rocket League rosters. It preserves results, player statistics, single-game records, standout achievements, and links to the original Ballchasing replays.

The recorded archive begins in Spring 2026. These are records for the games preserved in this archive—not a claim about every SHSU Rocket League match ever played. Historical seasons with missing replay coverage are not reconstructed from assumptions.

The record book tracks SHSU players and rosters. Opponent player statistics are used only for the separate standout methodology sample below, so observed rarity reflects all six players in a lobby rather than only our side.

FROM REPLAYS TO THE RECORD BOOK

How the data is collected & published

The website is a curated record of saved Rocket League replay files, not a live feed of every match. Replays are uploaded and organized into groups on Ballchasing.com. The program's master collection is available here:

View the SHSU 2026–2027 master replay group on Ballchasing ↗

  1. Save and group replays. Recorded SHSU matches are uploaded to Ballchasing and organized by series and competition. Each series recap also links to its relevant original Ballchasing group or replays.
  2. Export the player-games CSV. Ballchasing's players-games CSV export supplies a separate statistical row for each player's appearance in each replay, alongside the replay identifier and match information. These CSV files are archived with the project.
  3. Import SHSU statistics into SQLite. A Python parser (scripts/import_series.py) reads the CSV, matches appearances to predefined SHSU players and aliases already in the record-book database, and inserts the relevant series, game, roster, and player-game information into SQLite. It keeps the core statistics needed for records, achievements, and player profiles, such as score, goals, assists, saves, shots, and demolitions. It does not create public opponent career profiles.
  4. Generate static website pages. A separate Python exporter (scripts/export_static.py) renders the Flask/Jinja record-book pages from SQLite into static HTML and copies the site's styles and scripts. Those generated pages are published on GitHub Pages, allowing free, reliable hosting without a continuously running Flask server or a public SQLite backend.

The record book intentionally stores core SHSU record-tracking statistics, not every metric captured in a replay. For opposing players' box scores, positional or boost data, and deeper match analysis, follow the relevant series' Ballchasing links or the master replay group. Official forfeits are recorded in series results but do not generate fabricated player statistics.

NINE DEFINED THRESHOLDS

What is a standout?

A standout does not necessarily identify the best player or best overall performance in a match. Instead, the nine categories recognize different exceptional statistical performances, reveal how often those performances recur across a player's season or recorded career, and help describe how a player contributed. A player can have an excellent game without reaching a threshold, and a standout can occur in a loss.

A standout is one player appearance in one recorded game meeting at least one of the nine criteria below. The same rules apply to every appearance. These are descriptive milestones—not skill ratings, win awards, or complete measures of performance.

WHAT THE RESULTS SUGGEST

Insights from the sample

💥 Elite Disruptor and ⚓ Anchor were the rarest

Elite Disruptor occurred in 30 player-games (2.16%), and Anchor in 32 (2.30%). They were much less common than the 550+ Score threshold at 13.58%.

⚖️ SHSU and opponents had nearly identical overall rates

SHSU qualified on 22.13% of player-games, versus 22.41% for opponents—a gap of just 0.28 percentage points in this sample.

🧤 Opposing players earned Anchor more often

Opponents earned 23 Anchors (3.30%), compared with 9 for SHSU (1.29%). Matchups and defensive workload may affect this; it is not a direct measure of who defended better.

⭐ Most standout performances earned multiple categories

176 of 310 standout player-games (56.77%) earned two or more categories. The other 134 earned exactly one. Overall, 622 category awards were earned across those 310 performances.

👑 All-Around never occurred alone

All 43 All-Around occurrences overlapped with at least one other category. Under these exact nine thresholds, seven combined goals, assists, and saves necessarily trigger another category as well. All-Around is therefore mainly a descriptor of an exceptionally strong overall stat line in a game that already qualifies as a standout through another achievement.

🎯 Not every unusual or winning performance is a standout

Only 32 of 232 games (13.79%) had exactly one total category award anywhere in the lobby. Standouts are specific statistical thresholds, not win awards or comprehensive performance grades.

Insights above describe the specific 232 archived SHSU games and 1,392 player-game observations from Spring 2026 through November 4, 2026 (analyzed November 5, 2026). They are not universal collegiate averages or forecasts for unrelated skill levels and formats.

A SEPARATE, READ-ONLY STATISTICAL ANALYSIS

Standout sample methodology & limitations

The observed standout rates above are from a separate one-time analysis of archived Ballchasing player-games CSVs, not from the SHSU-only player statistics stored in the website's SQLite database. This allowed the sample to include both teams in every complete replay without importing opposing players into the record book.

  1. Snapshot and coverage. The current published snapshot was analyzed on November 5, 2026, using archived matches from Spring 2026 through November 4, 2026. The recursive scan recognized 63 Ballchasing player-games CSVs.
  2. Unique, complete replays only. The script deduplicated by replay ID, removed 7 duplicate replay copies, and retained 232 complete 3v3 replays with exactly three SHSU and three opposing player appearances. Unplayed forfeits do not enter the sample. No conflicting exports or invalid replays were excluded in this run.
  3. Identify SHSU versus opponent. Existing SHSU player aliases from the read-only SQLite database and reliable CSV team labels classify the six player rows. Four non-fatal team-label inconsistencies were resolved using the known aliases.
  4. Apply nine consistent rules. The same thresholds are evaluated for all 1,392 player-game observations (696 SHSU and 696 opponents). Each player-game qualifies for Any Standout once at most, but can earn multiple different categories.
  5. Measure different denominators. Category and player standout rates divide by player-games; six-player lobby rates divide by 232 unique games. The number of categories per standout player-game uses 310 qualifying player-games as its denominator. These are descriptive observed rates, not independent-event probabilities.
  6. Publish a fixed report. The analysis writes summary and detailed report files; the observed figures are then included on this page. New series imports do not automatically refresh these percentages. A new analysis snapshot would be needed to update them.
Interpretation and representativeness

This is an observed university program sample of SHSU's archived competitive matches, including selected opponents and formats. The games were not randomly sampled from all colleges, skill levels, or Rocket League playlists. These frequencies are not representative of all collegiate Rocket League and should not be interpreted as the average probability of a standout across every skill level or competition format. Defensive workload, opponents, playstyle, match length, and scoring conditions can all affect whether a threshold is reached.

Snapshot summary: Spring 2026–November 4, 2026 · analyzed November 5, 2026 · 232 complete games · 1,392 player-games (696 SHSU, 696 opponent). This scope applies to every frequency and distribution above; the site's SHSU record books themselves may contain newer data after subsequent imports.
